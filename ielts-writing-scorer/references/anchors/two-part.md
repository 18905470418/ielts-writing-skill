Compounding this, the countries now receiving the largest volumes of packaging are frequently those whose waste systems were built for a different, mostly organic, waste stream and were never funded to expand at the speed consumption did.

If the gap is the cause, closing it is the solution. Extended producer responsibility — obliging the company that puts packaging on the market to pay for its recovery — converts an externality into a line on a balance sheet, and firms respond to costs on balance sheets in a way they do not respond to appeals. Deposit schemes work by the same logic from the other end, giving a discarded bottle enough residual value that somebody will trouble to return it.

The objection is that these costs are ultimately passed to consumers, and they are. But they are being paid already, in fisheries, tourism and clean-up budgets, by people who had no part in the transaction. Moving that cost back to the point of sale does not create it. It simply puts it where a decision can be made about it.
