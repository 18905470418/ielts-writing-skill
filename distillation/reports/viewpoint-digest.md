  - A student who has never encountered economics, philosophy, or computer science cannot meaningfully choose to pursue or avoid them.

### `B9-ADV-027` · 9.0 · adv-disadv · cdieltsprep.com
- Q: Some cities have made all public transport free to use, paying for it through taxation instead of fares. Do you think this is a positive or negative development?
  - The case for it deserves to be stated fairly.
  - Fares are regressive: a monthly pass is a trivial sum to a lawyer and a serious one to a cleaner, and for people on low incomes the price of travel can determine which jobs are within reach at all.
  - Free travel also removes the cost of printing, selling and inspecting tickets, and in a small city where fares raise relatively little, that saving can go a long way towards paying for the policy.
  - The trouble is that price is seldom what keeps people off public transport.
  - What keeps them in their cars is time, reliability and the absence of a service at the hours they need one; a free bus that comes every forty minutes is still a bus that comes every forty minutes.
  - Removing fares therefore mostly rewards people who were already travelling, while the fare revenue that might have paid for more frequent services has gone.
